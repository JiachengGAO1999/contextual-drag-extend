Iterative LLM workflows retain previous attempts to support revision after feedback. Yet failed attempts can reduce accuracy on subsequent solutions, a phenomenon known as contextual drag. We ask whether this interference depends on continued access to the failed attempt after the model has processed it and received explicit error feedback. We introduce a same-text attention intervention that preserves the full prompt, token positions, prefill computation, and first-token logits, while blocking only later generated queries from attending to the failed draft. On 23 selected Game24 questions, Qwen3-8B accuracy falls from 87.0% in clean context to 39.1% with full access to a failed draft, then recovers to 78.3% when later access is blocked, a +39.1 percentage-point effect (95% question-cluster bootstrap interval: +23.9 to +54.3). Additional models and two MATH settings show the same direction. Blocking verified correct history instead lowers accuracy, showing that the access effect depends on the retained history and its feedback. These results identify a causal contribution of continued direct access to contextual drag after full initial exposure and explicit error feedback.
